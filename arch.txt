Our framework is a hybrid CNN-Transformer architecture for EEG signal classification that processes multi-channel brain signals through three main stages. The input first passes through a Multi-Scale Convolutional Embedding module containing three parallel temporal convolution branches with varying kernel sizes (15, 31, 63 samples) to capture both fast transient and slow oscillatory patterns, each normalized with Instance Normalization for cross-subject robustness. These multi-scale features are concatenated and processed by grouped depthwise spatial convolutions that learn electrode-specific spatial filters. A Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) is then applied, consisting of a Channel Attention sub-module that uses global average and max pooling with shared MLPs to weight frequency-band importance, followed by a Spatial Attention sub-module that highlights discriminative electrode regions through concatenated pooling and convolution. The attended features are projected and rearranged into a sequence of patch embeddings, which are fed into a stack of three Transformer Encoder blocks, each containing multi-head self-attention with 5 heads and a feed-forward network with GELU activation, both wrapped in residual connections with pre-LayerNorm. Finally, a Classification Head flattens the transformer outputs and passes them through a regularized MLP with progressive dimensionality reduction (2480→128→32→3) and high dropout rates to prevent subject-specific memorization, outputting class probabilities for three mental states.
